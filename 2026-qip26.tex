\begin{abstract}
In the projection lattice of a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, an orthogonal
basis---a \emph{context}---is characterised by the property that its atomic
probabilities sum to one on every state.  We study a weakening of this
condition in which two tuples of atoms induce the same probability sum on
every state.  Such pairs are called \emph{pseudocontexts}.  In finite
dimension this is equivalent to two distinct rank-one decompositions of the
same positive operator on its support, placing the notion naturally among
finite frame-theoretic constructions.  Size $k=1$ is trivial; size $k=2$
already admits genuinely nonorthogonal examples over both $\mathbb{R}$ and
$\mathbb{C}$; and, in fact, examples exist for every $k\ge 2$.  We present
the operator formulation, give a general explicit construction, analyse the
spectral structure of the associated structure operator, illustrate the
symmetric $k=3$ case, and list the remaining classification problems.
\end{abstract}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Let $L$ be the lattice of projections in a Hilbert space of finite dimension
$n\ge 3$.  Its atoms (minimal non-zero elements) correspond to one-dimensional
subspaces, represented by nonzero vectors.  States are described by
probability measures $P\colon L\to[0,1]$.  We denote by $S(L)$ the set of
all states on~$L$.

Vectors $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ form a \emph{context} if they compose an orthogonal
basis.  In this case (and only in this case) they satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \forall P\in S(L):\quad \sum_{i=1}^{n}P(a_i)=1.
  \label{eq1}
\end{equation}
The present note concerns weaker additive identifications obtained by
comparing two tuples of atoms that are not required to be orthogonal.  Such
\emph{pseudocontexts} are quantum-logical shadows of orthomodular-lattice
constructions of Mayet--Navara--Rogalewicz \cite{mayet2000} and have direct
physical meaning: they encode state-independent empirical equivalences between
families of generally noncommuting observables.  The answer is simple:
pseudocontexts already exist for $k=2$, and, more generally, for every
$k\ge 2$.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Preliminaries}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Throughout, $H$ is a complex Hilbert space with $\dim H=n\ge 3$, and
$L=L(H)$ is the orthomodular lattice of its closed subspaces.  Its atoms
correspond to one-dimensional subspaces, or equivalently to \emph{rays}
spanned by nonzero vectors.  We identify an atom with any representative unit
vector $a$ and write $P_a=|a\rangle\langle a|$ for the orthogonal projection
onto the ray it spans.

A \emph{state} is a map $P\colon L\to[0,1]$ with $P(I)=1$ that is finitely
additive on orthogonal families.  By Gleason's theorem \cite{gleason1957},
every state arises from a density operator $\rho$ via
\begin{equation}
  P(a)=\mathrm{tr}(\rho P_a),\qquad \rho\ge 0,\quad\mathrm{tr}\,\rho=1.
  \label{eq2}
\end{equation}
The key consequence we use repeatedly is that two self-adjoint operators that
agree on every state are equal as operators.

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Contexts and pseudocontexts}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

The operator form of \eqref{eq1} is elementary and is the starting point for
everything that follows.

\begin{proposition}[Contexts]\label{prop1}
Unit vectors $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in H$ form an orthonormal basis if and only if
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{a_i}=I.
  \label{eq3}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The forward implication is immediate.  For the converse, fix $j$ and evaluate
\eqref{eq3} on the vector $a_j$:
\[
  1=\langle a_j,I\,a_j\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\langle a_i,a_j\rangle|^2.
\]
The term with $i=j$ already contributes $1$, so all others must vanish.
Hence $\langle a_i,a_j\rangle=0$ for $i\neq j$.  As this holds for every
$j$, the family is orthonormal, and being of size $n$ in an $n$-dimensional
space it is an orthonormal basis.
\end{proof}

Combined with \eqref{eq2}, Proposition~\ref{prop1} recovers \eqref{eq1} as a
state-theoretic characterisation of orthogonal bases.

\begin{definition}[Pseudocontext]\label{def2}
Let $k\ge 1$.  A \emph{pseudocontext} of size $k$ is a pair of $k$-tuples of
atoms $(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$, $(b_1,\ldots,b_k)$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)]  each tuple contains pairwise distinct atoms,
  \item[(ii)] the two tuples are disjoint as sets of rays, so that
              $|\{a_1,\ldots,a_k,b_1,\ldots,b_k\}|=2k$, and
  \item[(iii)] $\displaystyle\forall P\in S(L):\quad
               \sum_{i=1}^{k}P(a_i)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}P(b_i)$.
              \label{eq4}
\end{enumerate}
A pseudocontext is called \emph{genuine} if neither $k$-tuple is a context.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
Condition~(i) excludes repeated atoms within a single tuple; condition~(ii)
ensures the two tuples are truly distinct.  Both are part of the definition;
without them the notion degenerates trivially.
\end{remark}

By \eqref{eq2}, condition~(iii) of Definition~\ref{def2} is equivalent to
the operator identity
\begin{equation}
  T:=\sum_{i=1}^{k}P_{a_i}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}P_{b_i},
  \label{eq5}
\end{equation}
which we call the \emph{structure operator} of the pseudocontext.  Thus
pseudocontexts are precisely pairs of distinct rank-one decompositions of a
single positive operator on its support.  This places them naturally among
finite frame-theoretic constructions: contexts are the special case in which
the structure operator is the identity on the relevant subspace; in general,
pseudocontexts are rank-one decompositions of a common positive operator.  If
the common operator is proportional to the identity on its support, the family
is a tight frame; after normalisation on that support one obtains a POVM\@.

\begin{remark}[Connection to SIC-POVMs]
When the common structure operator satisfies $T=\frac{k}{n}I$ (tight frame on
all of $H$), each family $\{P_{a_i}\}$ and $\{P_{b_i}\}$ scaled by $n/k$
forms a symmetric informationally complete POVM (SIC-POVM) if $k=n^2$ and
the frame vectors are equiangular \cite{zauner1999,renes2004}.  Pseudocontexts
therefore generalise the SIC-POVM notion by dropping both the tightness and
equiangularity requirements.
\end{remark}

\section{The smallest cases}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{proposition}[Size $k=1$]\label{prop3}
There is no pseudocontext of size $k=1$.  This holds for every $n\ge 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The condition $P(a_1)=P(b_1)$ for all $P\in S(L)$ is equivalent, via
\eqref{eq2}, to $P_{a_1}=P_{b_1}$.  This rank-one identity forces $a_1$ and
$b_1$ to span the same ray, contradicting distinctness condition~(ii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Every pseudocontext of size $k\ge 2$ can be realised in a two-dimensional
subspace (and hence embedded into $\mathbb{C}^n$ for any $n\ge 2$): all
constructions in Sections~\ref{sec:k2} and~\ref{sec:allk} live in
$\mathrm{span}\{e_1,e_2\}$.  The case $\mathbb{C}^1$ is excluded since every
unit vector spans the unique ray, making the distinctness condition
$|\{a_i\}\cup\{b_i\}|=2k$ impossible.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Pseudocontexts of size \texorpdfstring{$k=2$}{k=2}}
\label{sec:k2}

\begin{corollary}[Size $k=2$, complex]\label{cor4}
There are genuinely nonorthogonal pseudocontexts of size $k=2$ in $\mathbb{C}^n$
for every $n\ge 2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Embed $\mathbb{C}^2$ into $\mathbb{C}^n$ as the span of the first two basis
vectors and set
\[
  u_\pm=\Bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\pm\tfrac{1}{2},0,\ldots,0\Bigr),\qquad
  v_\pm=\Bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\pm\tfrac{i}{2},0,\ldots,0\Bigr).
\]
Then
\[
  P_{u_+}+P_{u_-}=P_{v_+}+P_{v_-}=
  \begin{pmatrix}3/2&0\\0&1/2\end{pmatrix}\oplus 0_{n-2}.
\]
The four rays are distinct.  Within each pair, $\langle u_+,u_-\rangle
=-\tfrac{1}{4}\neq 0$ and $\langle v_+,v_-\rangle=\tfrac{1}{4}\neq 0$, so
neither pair is orthogonal.  Hence neither pair is a context, and the
pseudocontext is genuine.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Real pseudocontexts of size $k=2$]\label{rem5}
Genuine pseudocontexts of size $k=2$ exist over \emph{real} Hilbert spaces as
well.  In $\mathbb{R}^2$ consider the operator $T=\mathrm{diag}(3/2,1/2)$ and
the two decompositions
\[
  u_\pm=\Bigl(\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2},\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigr),\qquad
  v_\pm=\Bigl(\tfrac{1}{2},\pm\tfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\Bigr).
\]
One checks directly that $P_{u_+}+P_{u_-}=P_{v_+}+P_{v_-}=T$, the four rays
are distinct, and $\langle u_+,u_-\rangle=-\tfrac{1}{4}\neq 0$, so the
pseudocontext is genuine.  Thus complex phases are \emph{not} necessary for
$k=2$; they merely provide one convenient route to the construction.  For
$k\ge 3$, real pseudocontexts also exist, as demonstrated by
Example~\ref{ex8}.
\end{remark}

\section{Pseudocontexts of every size \texorpdfstring{$k\ge 2$}{k>=2}}
\label{sec:allk}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[Complex-phase construction]\label{thm6}
Let $H$ contain an orthonormal pair $e_1,e_2$.  Fix an integer $k\ge 2$, let
$\omega=e^{2\pi i/k}$, choose $\alpha\in(0,\pi/2)$, and choose $\phi\in\mathbb{R}$
such that $e^{i\phi}$ is not a $k$th root of unity.  Define unit vectors
\[
  a_j=\cos\alpha\,e_1+\sin\alpha\,\omega^j e_2,\qquad
  b_j=\cos\alpha\,e_1+\sin\alpha\,e^{i\phi}\omega^j e_2,\qquad
  j=0,\ldots,k-1.
\]
Then the $2k$ rays spanned by the $a_j$ and $b_j$ are pairwise distinct, and
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}P_{a_j}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}P_{b_j}
  =k\bigl(\cos^2\alpha\,P_{e_1}+\sin^2\alpha\,P_{e_2}\bigr).
\]
Hence $(a_0,\ldots,a_{k-1})$ and $(b_0,\ldots,b_{k-1})$ form a pseudocontext
of size $k$.  If $\alpha\neq\pi/4$, then the pseudocontext is genuine.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write $c=\cos\alpha$ and $s=\sin\alpha$.  For each $j$,
\[
  P_{a_j}=c^2 P_{e_1}+s^2 P_{e_2}
          +cs\bigl(\omega^{-j}|e_1\rangle\langle e_2|
                  +\omega^{j}|e_2\rangle\langle e_1|\bigr),
\]
and
\[
  P_{b_j}=c^2 P_{e_1}+s^2 P_{e_2}
          +cs\bigl(e^{-i\phi}\omega^{-j}|e_1\rangle\langle e_2|
                  +e^{i\phi}\omega^{j}|e_2\rangle\langle e_1|\bigr).
\]
Summing over $j=0,\ldots,k-1$ and using $\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\omega^j=0$ gives
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{k-1}P_{a_j}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}P_{b_j}
  =k\bigl(c^2 P_{e_1}+s^2 P_{e_2}\bigr).
\]

\textit{Distinctness.}
Suppose $a_j=\lambda a_\ell$ for some scalar $\lambda$.  Since $c\neq 0$,
comparing first coordinates gives $\lambda=1$, and then $\omega^j=\omega^\ell$,
so $j=\ell$ (among $\{0,\ldots,k-1\}$).  Hence the $a_j$ are pairwise
distinct rays.  The identical argument shows the $b_j$ are pairwise distinct
rays.  If $a_j=\lambda b_\ell$, then $\lambda=1$ and $\omega^j=e^{i\phi}\omega^\ell$,
i.e.\ $e^{i\phi}=\omega^{j-\ell}$, which contradicts the assumption that
$e^{i\phi}$ is not a $k$th root of unity.  Hence no $a_j$ coincides with any
$b_\ell$.

\textit{Genuineness.}
For $j\neq\ell$ within the $a$-tuple,
\[
  \langle a_j,a_\ell\rangle=c^2+s^2\omega^{\ell-j}.
\]
This vanishes only if $c^2=s^2$ (i.e.\ $\alpha=\pi/4$) and
$\omega^{\ell-j}=-1$.  So for $\alpha\neq\pi/4$ no two distinct vectors in
the $a$-tuple are orthogonal.  An identical computation gives
$\langle b_j,b_\ell\rangle=c^2+s^2\omega^{\ell-j}$, so the same conclusion
holds for the $b$-tuple.  Hence neither tuple is a context when
$\alpha\neq\pi/4$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem7}
At the special value $\alpha=\pi/4$, the structure operator becomes
$\frac{k}{2}I_{\mathrm{span}\{e_1,e_2\}}$, so the construction yields a
unit-norm tight frame on the two-dimensional support.  For $k=2$ this reduces
to a context; for $k\ge 3$ it gives a highly symmetric pseudocontext
(cf.\ Remark~\ref{rem9}).
\end{remark}

\section{A symmetric \texorpdfstring{$k=3$}{k=3} example}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

The case $k=3$ admits a very transparent planar representation; it is the
finite-dimensional analogue of the Mercedes--Benz frame.

\begin{example}[Mercedes--Benz type pseudocontext]\label{ex8}
Fix a two-dimensional subspace $V\subset H$ with orthonormal basis $(e_1,e_2)$,
and fix an angle $\theta\in(0,\pi)\setminus\{0,\pi/3,2\pi/3,\pi\}$ (modulo
$\pi$).  Put, for $j=0,1,2$,
\[
  a_j=\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2\pi j}{3}\Bigr)e_1
     +\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2\pi j}{3}\Bigr)e_2,\qquad
  b_j=\cos\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2\pi j}{3}+\theta\Bigr)e_1
     +\sin\!\Bigl(\tfrac{2\pi j}{3}+\theta\Bigr)e_2.
\]
Then the six rays are pairwise distinct, and
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{2}P_{a_j}=\sum_{j=0}^{2}P_{b_j}=\tfrac{3}{2}I_V.
\]
Thus $\{a_0,a_1,a_2\}$ and $\{b_0,b_1,b_2\}$ form a genuine pseudocontext of
size $k=3$ over $\mathbb{R}$.
\end{example}

\begin{proof}
In the basis $(e_1,e_2)$, each projector has the form
\[
  P_{\cos\varphi\,e_1+\sin\varphi\,e_2}
  =\begin{pmatrix}\cos^2\varphi&\cos\varphi\sin\varphi\\
                  \cos\varphi\sin\varphi&\sin^2\varphi\end{pmatrix}.
\]
For $\varphi_j=2\pi j/3$ and for $\varphi_j=\theta+2\pi j/3$, the
trigonometric identities
\[
  \sum_{j=0}^{2}\cos(2\varphi_j)=0,\qquad
  \sum_{j=0}^{2}\sin(2\varphi_j)=0
\]
imply that the off-diagonal terms cancel while the diagonal terms each sum to
$3/2$.  Hence both sums equal $\frac{3}{2}I_V$.

The exclusion $\theta\notin\{0,\pi/3,2\pi/3,\pi\}$ (modulo $\pi$) is needed
to guarantee that no vector $b_j$ coincides with any $a_\ell$ or with
$-a_\ell$ (same ray).  Specifically: $\theta=0$ gives $b_j=a_j$; $\theta=\pi$
gives $b_j=-a_j$ (same ray); $\theta=\pi/3$ maps the $b$-triple onto the
$a$-triple (as a set); and $\theta=2\pi/3$ similarly identifies the two sets
with a permutation.

Since $c^2\neq s^2$ at $\alpha=\pi/4$ is not applicable here (all vectors
have unit norm in $\mathbb{R}^2$ with $c=\cos(2\pi j/3)$ varying), neither
triple consists of mutually orthogonal vectors for generic $\theta$, so the
pseudocontext is genuine.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Complex trine description]\label{rem9}
Example~\ref{ex8} has a compact complex analogue.  Let $\omega=e^{2\pi i/3}$
and embed $V$ into $\mathbb{C}^n$ as $\mathrm{span}\{e_1,e_2\}$.  The
\emph{trines}
\[
  u_j=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(1,\omega^j,0,\ldots,0\bigr),\qquad
  v_j=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(1,e^{i\phi}\omega^j,0,\ldots,0\bigr),\qquad
  j=0,1,2,
\]
yield the same phenomenon after a change of basis in $V$.  The identity
$1+\omega+\omega^2=0$ makes the off-diagonal entries of $\sum_j P_{u_j}$
cancel, giving the common structure operator $\frac{3}{2}I_V$.  This form is
most natural in frame-theoretic discussions and in connection with the
Weyl--Heisenberg group.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Nondegenerate structure operators]\label{rem10}
The trine family is highly symmetric: its structure operator is a scalar
multiple of the identity on its support.  More complicated examples with
nondegenerate structure operators occur already in dimension three; see, for
instance, the coordinatisations discussed in \cite{navara2024form,
navara2025exploring}.  Such examples are useful when one wants to move beyond
purely symmetric frame orbits.
\end{remark}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Spectral structure and canonical forms}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

We analyse the general properties of the structure operator
\[
  T=\sum_{i=1}^{k}P_{a_i}
\]
associated with a $k$-tuple of unit vectors $(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$ in $H$.

\subsection{Gram representation and spectral equivalence}

\begin{definition}[Analysis operator and Gram matrix]
Let $A\colon\mathbb{C}^k\to H$ be defined by $Ae_i=a_i$, where $(e_i)$ is
the standard basis of $\mathbb{C}^k$.  The \emph{analysis operator} is
$A^\dagger\colon H\to\mathbb{C}^k$, and the \emph{Gram matrix} is
\[
  G:=A^\dagger A=\bigl(\langle a_i,a_j\rangle\bigr)_{i,j=1}^k.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Frame operator factorisation]\label{prop:AAstar}
The structure operator admits the factorisation $T=AA^\dagger$, and $G=A^\dagger A$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral equivalence]\label{prop:spectral_equivalence}
The nonzero spectra of $T$ and $G$ coincide (including multiplicities):
\[
  \sigma(T)\setminus\{0\}=\sigma(G)\setminus\{0\}.
\]
In particular,
\[
  \mathrm{rank}(T)=\mathrm{rank}(G)\le\min(k,n).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
This is the standard singular-value correspondence between $AA^\dagger$ and
$A^\dagger A$: if $AA^\dagger v=\lambda v$ with $\lambda\neq 0$, then
$A^\dagger A(A^\dagger v)=\lambda(A^\dagger v)$ and $A^\dagger v\neq 0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Spectral constraints}

\begin{proposition}[Trace identities]\label{prop:trace}
The structure operator satisfies
\[
  \mathrm{tr}(T)=k,\qquad
  \mathrm{tr}(T^2)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{k}|\langle a_i,a_j\rangle|^2.
\]
The diagonal terms $i=j$ each contribute $1$, giving $\mathrm{tr}(T^2)\ge k$,
with equality if and only if the vectors are mutually orthogonal.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{tr}(T)=\sum_i\mathrm{tr}(P_{a_i})=k$.  For the second identity,
$\mathrm{tr}(T^2)=\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{tr}(P_{a_i}P_{a_j})=\sum_{i,j}|\langle
a_i,a_j\rangle|^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Non-orthogonality and spectral spread]\label{prop:strict}
If the vectors $(a_i)$ are not mutually orthogonal, then
\[
  \mathrm{tr}(T^2)>k.
\]
Equivalently, the spectrum of $T$ is non-flat and $\lambda_{\max}>1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Majorisation constraint]\label{prop:maj}
Suppose $k\le n$.  Let $\lambda(T)=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_n)$ be the
eigenvalues of $T$ in decreasing order (padded with zeros), and let
$d=(1,\ldots,1,0,\ldots,0)$ be the vector with $k$ ones and $n-k$ zeros.
Then
\[
  \lambda(T)\succ d.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The diagonal entries of the Gram matrix $G$ are $G_{ii}=\|a_i\|^2=1$.  By
the Schur--Horn theorem, the vector of diagonal entries of $G$ is majorised by
the vector of its eigenvalues.  Since $G$ and $T$ share the same nonzero
eigenvalues (Proposition~\ref{prop:spectral_equivalence}), the eigenvalue
vector of $T$ (padded with $n-k$ zeros when $k\le n$) majorises
$d=(1,\ldots,1,0,\ldots,0)$.

\emph{Note on the overcomplete case $k>n$.}  When $k>n$ the Gram matrix $G$
is $k\times k$ with rank at most $n$; the majorisation statement must then be
formulated for the $k$-dimensional eigenvalue vector of $G$ against
$(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^k$, and the above argument applies to $G$ in place
of $T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Operator bounds]\label{prop:bounds}
The structure operator satisfies
\[
  0\le T\le kI.
\]
Moreover, if $\mu:=\max_{i\neq j}|\langle a_i,a_j\rangle|$, then
\[
  \lambda_{\max}(T)\le 1+(k-1)\mu.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Positivity $T\ge 0$ is immediate, and $T\le kI$ since each $P_{a_i}\le I$.
For the sharper bound, apply the Ger\v{s}gorin circle theorem to the Gram
matrix $G$: since $G_{ii}=1$ and $|G_{ij}|\le\mu$ for $i\neq j$, every
eigenvalue of $G$ lies in a disk centred at $1$ with radius
$\sum_{j\neq i}|G_{ij}|\le(k-1)\mu$.  Because the nonzero eigenvalues of $T$
and $G$ coincide, $\lambda_{\max}(T)\le 1+(k-1)\mu$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Commutant and symmetry}

\begin{definition}[Commutant]
Let
\[
  \mathcal{C}(T):=\{U\in U(H):UT=TU\}
\]
be the unitary commutant of $T$.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Structure of the commutant]\label{prop:commutant}
Let $T=\sum_\alpha\lambda_\alpha P_\alpha$ be the spectral decomposition of
$T$ with distinct eigenvalues $\lambda_\alpha$ and orthogonal projections
$P_\alpha$ of ranks $m_\alpha$.  Then
\[
  \mathcal{C}(T)\cong\prod_\alpha U(m_\alpha).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
Spectral degeneracies ($m_\alpha>1$) give rise to continuous families of
distinct rank-one decompositions of $T$ (as in tight frames), whereas simple
spectrum leads to strong rigidity.  This explains why the tight-frame
(scalar-multiple-of-identity) case in Remark~\ref{rem7} is so rich in
pseudocontexts.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Canonical normal form of the structure operator}

\begin{proposition}[Spectral normal form]\label{prop:normalform}
Every structure operator is unitarily equivalent to a diagonal operator
\[
  T\cong\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r,0,\ldots,0),
\]
where $\lambda_i>0$, $\sum_{i=1}^r\lambda_i=k$, and $r=\mathrm{rank}(T)$.
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Canonical parametrisation of all decompositions}

We now describe all rank-one decompositions of a fixed positive operator $T$.

\begin{theorem}[Canonical decomposition via the analysis operator]
\label{thm:canonical}
Let $T$ be a positive operator of rank $r$ with spectral decomposition
\[
  T=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_i|e_i\rangle\langle e_i|,\quad\lambda_i>0,
\]
and set $\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_r)$.

Every rank-one decomposition $T=\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{a_j}$ with $\|a_j\|=1$ is
obtained as follows.  There exists a matrix $C\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times k}$ such
that
\begin{equation}
  CC^\dagger=\Lambda,\qquad \|c_j\|^2=1\text{ for all }j,
  \label{eq:Ccond}
\end{equation}
where $c_j$ is the $j$-th column of $C$, and $a_j=\sum_{i=1}^{r}c_{ij}e_i$.
Conversely, every matrix $C$ satisfying \eqref{eq:Ccond} defines a valid
decomposition.

A necessary condition for such a $C$ to exist is that $\lambda_i\le k-r+1$
for all $i$ \cite{casazza2006}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $T=AA^\dagger$ be any factorisation with columns $a_j$.  In the
eigenbasis of $T$, write $A=UC$ where $U$ diagonalises $T$ (restricted to
$\mathrm{supp}(T)$).  Then $T=AA^\dagger=UCC^\dagger U^\dagger$, so
$CC^\dagger=\Lambda$.  The column-norm condition follows from $\|a_j\|=1$.
The converse is a direct computation.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Unitary freedom]\label{cor:unitary}
Among all rank-one decompositions of $T$ into $k$ (not necessarily unit-norm)
vectors, any two are related by right multiplication $A\mapsto AU$ for some
$U\in U(k)$.  Imposing the unit-norm constraint \eqref{eq:Ccond} cuts out a
submanifold of this orbit; in general, the $U(k)$-action is not transitive on
the unit-norm decompositions.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Characterisation of pseudocontexts}

\begin{proposition}[Operator equivalence criterion]\label{prop:pseudo_equiv}
Two $k$-tuples $(a_i)$ and $(b_i)$ of distinct rays form a pseudocontext if
and only if their corresponding matrices $C_A,C_B$ (expressed in the
eigenbasis of $T$) satisfy
\[
  C_A C_A^\dagger=C_B C_B^\dagger=\Lambda.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
Thus pseudocontexts correspond to distinct points in the space
\[
  \bigl\{C\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times k}:CC^\dagger=\Lambda,\,\|c_j\|=1\bigr\}/\!\sim,
\]
where $\sim$ identifies decompositions giving the same set of rays.  This is
the space of inequivalent rank-one decompositions of a fixed positive operator
with prescribed column norms.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Extremal spectral bounds}

\begin{proposition}[Rayleigh bounds]\label{prop:rayleigh}
For any density operator $\rho$,
\[
  \lambda_{\min}(T)\le\mathrm{tr}(\rho T)\le\lambda_{\max}(T).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
These bounds are tight (achieved by pure states supported on the eigenvectors
of $T$).  They determine the quantum range of the probability sums
$\sum_i P(a_i)$ that define the pseudocontext.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Examples of nondegenerate spectra}

Explicit three-dimensional implementations of nondegenerate structure operators
satisfying the bounds above were derived in \cite{navara2024form} in the study
of generalised gadget hypergraphs.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{15-atom hypergraph ($T_1$).}  A pseudocontext of size $k=3$
  generated from a generalised firefly logic yields a structure operator
  $T_1$ with eigenvalues
  \[
    \lambda_1=2,\quad
    \lambda_2=\tfrac{7+\sqrt{21}}{14}\approx 0.827,\quad
    \lambda_3=\tfrac{7-\sqrt{21}}{14}\approx 0.173.
  \]
  One checks $\mathrm{tr}(T_1)=3$ and $\lambda_{\max}=2>1$, consistent with
  Proposition~\ref{prop:strict}.  The Ger\v{s}gorin bound of
  Proposition~\ref{prop:bounds} is satisfied as well.

\item \textbf{36-atom hypergraph ($T_2$).}  A larger generalised
  true-implies-false gadget gives another $k=3$ pseudocontext.  Its structure
  operator is $T_2=\mathrm{diag}(1/5,1/5,13/5)$.  Here $\mathrm{tr}(T_2)=3$
  and the spatial frame is stretched anisotropically with $\lambda_{\max}=13/5>1$.
\end{itemize}

\section{Physical interpretation and constraints}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

A pseudocontext $(\{a_i\},\{b_i\})$ implements an empirical equivalence
between two families of rank-one observables $A_i=P_{a_i}$ and
$B_i=P_{b_i}$.  Although the operators within each family are generally
noncommuting, the two linear functionals
\[
  \rho\longmapsto\sum_i\mathrm{tr}(\rho A_i),\qquad
  \rho\longmapsto\sum_i\mathrm{tr}(\rho B_i)
\]
agree on the whole state space.  Because the structure operators are identical,
this equivalence is strictly state-independent.

The spectral decomposition of $T$ establishes absolute quantum limits on these
probability sums.  By the Rayleigh--Ritz theorem (Proposition~\ref{prop:rayleigh}),
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_{\min}(T)\le\sum_{i=1}^{k}P(a_i)\le\lambda_{\max}(T).
  \label{eq:bounds}
\end{equation}
These bounds are tight.  For the 36-atom hypergraph example \cite{navara2024form},
the structure operator $T_2=\mathrm{diag}(1/5,1/5,13/5)$ confines the quantum
probability sum to the interval $[1/5,\,13/5]=[0.2,\,2.6]$.  A classical
hidden-variable model based on two-valued ($\{0,1\}$) truth assignments to
projections---subject to the consistency requirement that exactly one atom in
each orthogonal basis receives the value $1$---would allow the sum to range
over the integer values $\{0,1,2,3\}$, which includes $0$ and $3$.  Because
$T_2$ has no eigenvalue equal to $0$ or $3$, quantum mechanics forbids these
extreme classical values; the quantum system cannot reach them.  This
discrepancy is a signature of Kochen--Specker-type contextuality
\cite{kochen1967}.

We note that this state-independent additive equivalence is related to, but
distinct from, the operational notion of contextuality introduced by Spekkens
\cite{spekkens2005}, which applies to arbitrary experimental procedures
(preparations, transformations, and unsharp measurements) rather than to sharp
projective measurements alone.  A precise comparison is beyond the scope of
this note.

In the language of quantum information theory, the collections $\{A_i\}$ and
$\{B_i\}$ are distinct rank-one operator decompositions of the same positive
operator.  If the common operator is proportional to the identity on its
support, they form a tight frame; after normalisation one obtains a POVM\@.
From a foundational standpoint, this is a state-independent additive
equivalence closely related to, but distinct from, Kochen--Specker-type
contextuality \cite{kochen1967,cabello1996}.

\section{Open problems}
% ------------------------------------------------------------------

Having established the existence of pseudocontexts for every size $k\ge 2$
and isolated a simple $k=3$ illustration, we highlight the remaining questions
regarding their deeper classification.

\paragraph{Open Problem (Classification).}
Classify, up to unitary equivalence, all pseudocontexts of size $k$ in
$\mathbb{C}^n$.  Even for small $(k,n)$, the orbit structure is not yet
transparent.

\paragraph{Open Problem (Lattice representability).}
Characterise which orthomodular lattices carrying a pseudocontext-type additive
identification admit a faithful embedding into some $L(\mathbb{C}^n)$,
sharpening the partial results of \cite{navara2024form,navara2025exploring}.

\paragraph{Open Problem (Frame-symmetry characterisation).}
Can one characterise pseudocontexts purely in terms of symmetries of the
underlying rank-one frame---for example, in terms of unitaries commuting with
the structure operator and permuting the atoms of the two decompositions?

\paragraph{Open Problem (Minimal ambient dimension).}
Although every pseudocontext of size $k\ge 2$ can be realised in dimension
$n=2$, what is the minimal $n$ for which \emph{all} pseudocontexts of a given
combinatorial type occur?  For Kochen--Specker configurations this question
has a long history; the pseudocontext analogue is open.
