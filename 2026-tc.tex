\title{All Correlations Yield the Same Peak Work,
       But Quantum is a Better Fuel}

\author{Karl Svozil\,}

\begin{abstract}
Initial system--environment correlations are a thermodynamic resource,
enabling work extraction via their erasure.
We compare the work potential of classical, quantum, and hypothetical
stronger-than-quantum correlations as a function of measurement
misalignment.
While all models can yield a peak extractable work of
$k_{\mathrm{B}} T \ln 2$, corresponding to a mutual information of
$\ln 2$ \blue{(the maximum for a single unbiased binary outcome)},
their value as a resource differs critically in robustness.
The classical resource is fragile, \blue{with its mutual information degrading
as $\Theta(\delta\theta\ln(1/\delta\theta))$ under a
misalignment~$\delta\theta$,} whereas the quantum resource is robust,
\blue{degrading only as $\Theta(\delta\theta^{2}\ln(1/\delta\theta))$.
One stronger-than-quantum resource is perfectly robust for all but a
single critical angle.}
This hierarchy in operational robustness is quantitatively expressed
through \blue{an aggregate thermodynamic robustness parameter
evaluated across the measurement settings of a CHSH-type Bell test}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% =====================================================

Classical thermodynamics defines an isolated system by boundaries
impermeable to matter and energy.
This picture is challenged by microscopic systems prepared with initial
correlations to their environment.
Even when sealed against energy exchange, such a system's local state
can be altered by operations on its correlated environmental
counterpart.
An agent possessing side information can consume these correlations to
perform work, a process governed by generalized thermodynamic laws that
incorporate information-theoretic
balances~\cite{Bera2017a,delrio2011thermodynamic,Sagawa2012}.

\blue{The connection between information and thermodynamics has a long
history, originating with Maxwell's ``demon'' thought experiment and
formalized through the work of Szilard~\cite{Szilard-1929}, who showed
that a single bit of information about a molecule's position in a
partitioned box can be converted into $k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2$ of work.
Landauer~\cite{landauer:61} later established that this cycle is closed
by the erasure cost: resetting the demon's memory dissipates at least
$k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2$ of heat, thereby saving the second
law~\cite{bennett-82,Maruyama2009}.

In the quantum regime, information-thermodynamic trade-offs are enriched
by genuinely non-classical correlations.
It has been shown that quantum
discord~\cite{Zurek2003,Oppenheim2002} and quantum
entanglement~\cite{delrio2011thermodynamic} can serve as thermodynamic
resources, and that the framework of generalized free
energies~\cite{Brandao2015,Brandao2015b,Horodecki2013} extends the
second law to the single-shot regime.
Sagawa and Ueda~\cite{SagawaUeda2008,sagawa2010} established
rigorous equalities connecting feedback control, measurement,
and work extraction in the quantum setting.
Comprehensive reviews of the field can be found in
Refs.~\cite{Goold2016,Vinjanampathy2016}.}

This paper demonstrates that the \emph{nonlocal character} of a
correlation law maps directly to its \emph{operational value} as a
thermodynamic resource.
We show that while classical, quantum, and stronger-than-quantum
correlations share the same peak energetic value, they exhibit a clear
hierarchy in their robustness against experimental imperfection.
\blue{This robustness hierarchy is quantified by an aggregate
thermodynamic parameter inspired by the measurement settings of the
Clauser--Horne--Shimony--Holt (CHSH)
scenario~\cite{chsh,bell,Brunner2014}, though we emphasize that this
parameter is an operational figure of merit, not a Bell inequality.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:prelim} reviews the information-theoretic framework
connecting mutual information to extractable work and derives the mutual
information for binary-outcome correlations.
Section~\ref{sec:protocol} describes the operational
work-extraction protocol.
Section~\ref{sec:hierarchy} introduces the three representative
correlation laws.
Section~\ref{sec:thermo_value} analyzes their thermodynamic value and
robustness, establishing a general smoothness criterion.
Section~\ref{sec:CHSH_work} defines and evaluates an aggregate
thermodynamic robustness parameter across multiple measurement
settings.
Section~\ref{sec:extensions} discusses extensions and limitations,
including the relaxation of the binary-outcome and uniform-marginal
assumptions.
In simple terms, a \textit{uniform marginal} means that if one looks at the results of just one party (``Alice alone'' or ``Bob alone'') and ignores the other,
their outcomes appear completely random and equally distributed---the output is indistinguishable from pure noise. It appears as a sequence of perfectly random coin flips ($+1$ or $-1$
with $50\%$ probability) that reveals absolutely no information on its own; the meaningful structure exists solely in the relationship between the two sides.
Section~\ref{sec:discussion} positions the work relative to the
existing literature.
Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} concludes.}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:prelim}
% =====================================================

\subsection{From information to work}
\label{sec:info_to_work}

\blue{Consider a bipartite system consisting of a subsystem~$A$ and its
environment~$E$, jointly described by a state $\rho_{AE}$ with reduced
states $\rho_A = \mathrm{Tr}_E[\rho_{AE}]$ and
$\rho_E = \mathrm{Tr}_A[\rho_{AE}]$.
The quantum mutual information,
\begin{equation}
I(A:E) = S(\rho_A) + S(\rho_E) - S(\rho_{AE}),
\label{eq:QMI}
\end{equation}
where $S(\rho) = -\mathrm{Tr}[\rho \ln \rho]$ is the von~Neumann
entropy, quantifies the total correlations (both classical and quantum)
between $A$ and~$E$.}

If $A$ is in contact with a thermal bath at temperature $T$, and the
agent performs a cyclic process on $A$ that begins with correlations
$I_i(A{:}E)$ and terminates in an uncorrelated product state
$\rho_A\otimes\rho_E$, the generalized second
law~\cite{Bera2017a} imposes the following bound on the extractable
work:
\blue{\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{ext}} \le -\Delta F_T(\rho_A)
  + k_{\mathrm{B}} T\, I_i(A{:}E),
\label{eq:gen_second_law}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta F_T(\rho_A)$ is the change in the non-equilibrium free
energy of subsystem~$A$.
This result can be understood intuitively: the mutual information
$I_i(A{:}E)$ represents a ``bonus'' resource, additional to the
system's own free energy, that an informed agent can exploit.}
When the process is cyclic on~$A$, so that $\Delta F_T(\rho_A)=0$,
this simplifies to
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{ext}} \le k_{\mathrm{B}} T\, I_i(A{:}E).
\label{eq:work_from_I}
\end{equation}
\blue{The physical content of Eq.~\eqref{eq:work_from_I} generalizes
Szilard's result~\cite{Szilard-1929}: one bit of mutual information
($I = \ln 2$~nats) can be reversibly converted into
$k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2$ of work, precisely the energy liberated by an
isothermal expansion of a single-molecule gas when the piston's initial
position is known.
Following del~Rio~\emph{et al.}~\cite{delrio2011thermodynamic}, the
work cost of erasing a system is governed by the conditional entropy
$S(A|E) = S(\rho_{AE}) - S(\rho_E)$, which can be negative for
entangled states, permitting work \emph{gain} during erasure.
The work bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:work_from_I} is consistent with this
picture, since for a maximally mixed marginal $\rho_A$ (entropy
$S(\rho_A) = \ln 2$), we have $I(A{:}E) = \ln 2 - S(A|E)$.

\emph{Scope of the bound.}
The quantum mutual information of Eq.~\eqref{eq:QMI} can exceed
$\ln 2$ for quantum subsystems (for instance,
$I(A{:}E) = 2\ln 2$ for a maximally entangled two-qubit state),
and in principle more general protocols could exploit such larger
correlations~\cite{delrio2011thermodynamic}.
In the present work we restrict attention to the
\emph{outcome-level} mutual information arising from binary
measurements with uniform marginals, for which
$I \le \ln 2$ always holds.
The bound $W \le k_{\mathrm{B}}T \ln 2$ is therefore specific to
this one-unbiased-bit operational setting, not universal across all
quantum thermodynamic protocols.}

\subsection{Mutual information from correlations}
\label{sec:MI_from_E}

We now specialize to a scenario directly relevant to Bell-type
experiments.
Consider two parties sharing a no-signaling bipartite system with binary
outcomes $a, e \in \{+1, -1\}$ and uniform marginals:
$P(a) = P(e) = 1/2$.
In this case, the joint distribution is entirely determined by the
correlation function
\begin{equation}
E(\theta)
  = \langle a\, e \rangle_\theta
  = \sum_{a,e} a\, e\, P(a,e|\theta),
\label{eq:corr_def}
\end{equation}
where $\theta$ is the relative angle between the two measurement
settings.
\blue{Explicitly, the joint probabilities are
\begin{equation}
P(a = e|\theta)   = \frac{1+E(\theta)}{2},
\qquad
P(a \neq e|\theta) = \frac{1-E(\theta)}{2}.
\label{eq:joint_probs}
\end{equation}
The marginal entropy is $H(A) = \ln 2$.
The conditional entropy is the binary entropy of the error probability,
\begin{equation}
H(A|E)
  = h_2\!\left(\frac{1-E(\theta)}{2}\right)
  = h_2\!\left(\frac{1+E(\theta)}{2}\right),
\end{equation}
where $h_2(p) = -p\ln p - (1{-}p)\ln(1{-}p)$ is the binary Shannon
entropy in nats, and the last equality follows from the symmetry
$h_2(p)=h_2(1{-}p)$.}
The mutual information is therefore
\begin{equation}
I_\theta(A{:}E) = H(A) - H(A|E) = \ln 2
  - h_2\!\left(\frac{1+E(\theta)}{2}\right).
\label{eq:Itheta}
\end{equation}
\blue{This result depends on the correlation $E(\theta)$ \emph{only through
its absolute value} $|E(\theta)|$, via the binary entropy.}
Perfect correlation or anti-correlation ($|E|=1$) gives $I = \ln 2$;
zero correlation ($E=0$) gives $I = 0$.
\blue{Note that Eq.~\eqref{eq:Itheta} is \emph{model-independent}: it holds for any bipartite device
(or theory) whose outputs are binary with uniform marginals, in which case
the joint distribution is fully determined by the correlator
$E(\theta)=\langle AE\rangle_\theta$.}

% =====================================================
\blue{\section{Operational Protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}}
% =====================================================

\blue{To make the work-extraction scenario concrete, we describe an
operational protocol inspired by the Szilard
engine~\cite{Szilard-1929,SagawaUeda2008}. We treat the measurement
outcomes as classical registers; hence $I_\theta(A{:}E)$ denotes the
\emph{Shannon} mutual information of the binary variables $A,E\in\{\pm1\}$.}

\blue{\begin{enumerate}
\item Alice and Bob share a correlated resource (classical shared
randomness, an entangled quantum state, or a no-signaling box).

\item A referee assigns measurement settings: Alice receives setting $x$
(direction $\alpha_x$) and Bob receives setting $y$ (direction $\beta_y$).
The settings (equivalently, the relative angle $\theta=\alpha_x-\beta_y$)
are assumed to be known to both parties (e.g., publicly announced by the
referee).

\item Alice performs her measurement and obtains $a\in\{+1,-1\}$.
Bob queries his device with setting $y$, obtaining $e\in\{+1,-1\}$, which
is stored in a physical two-state working medium (e.g., Left/Right of a
one-molecule Szilard box at temperature $T$).

\item Alice communicates her one-bit result $a$ to Bob via a classical
channel.

\item Using $a$ and the known correlator $E(\theta)=\langle AE\rangle_\theta$,
Bob performs a reversible relabeling (a conditional flip) of his two-state
working medium and defines an ``agreement'' variable
\[
z := \sigma(\theta)\, a e \in \{+1,-1\},
\]
where $\sigma(\theta)\in\{+1,-1\}$ is chosen to maximize $P(z=+1|\theta)$
(equivalently, $\sigma(\theta)=\mathrm{sgn}\,E(\theta)$ whenever
$E(\theta)\neq 0$). For uniform marginals,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
p_{\rm corr}(\theta) &= P(z=+1|\theta)  \\
&= \max\!\Big\{\frac{1+E(\theta)}{2},\frac{1-E(\theta)}{2}\Big\} \\
&= \frac{1+|E(\theta)|}{2}\,.
\end{split}
\label{eq:pcorr}
\end{equation}

\item Bob now performs a feedback-controlled quasi-static isothermal
protocol that brings his two-state working medium from the biased
distribution $P(z)$ to thermal equilibrium (uniform). The $A$--$E$
correlation is the informational resource being consumed. The maximum
average extractable work per run is~\cite{SagawaUeda2008,parrondo2015}
\begin{equation}
W(\theta) = k_{\mathrm{B}}T\,I_\theta(A{:}E)
= k_{\mathrm{B}}T\!\left[\ln 2
- h_2\!\left(\frac{1+|E(\theta)|}{2}\right)\right]\!.
\label{eq:wtheta}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}}

\blue{Since Alice communicates exactly one classical bit and the
marginals are uniform, the mutual information satisfies
$I_\theta(A{:}E) \le H(A) = \ln 2$, hence $W(\theta)\le k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2$
in this one-unbiased-bit setting~\cite{landauer:61,bennett-82}.
This bound is saturated when $|E(\theta)|=1$, i.e., when Bob can
deterministically predict (equivalently, deterministically relabel) the
logical state of his two-state working medium.}

\section{A Hierarchy of Correlation Laws}
\label{sec:hierarchy}
% =====================================================

We analyze three representative bipartite correlation laws $E(\theta)$
for $\{\pm1\}$-outcome measurements as a function of the relative
angle $\theta \in [0, \pi]$ between settings.
Their nonlocality is gauged by the CHSH
parameter~\cite{chsh,bell}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{CHSH}}
  = |E(\theta_1,\theta_2) + E(\theta_1,\theta_2')
    + E(\theta_1',\theta_2) - E(\theta_1',\theta_2')|,
\end{equation}
for which local realism imposes a bound of~2, quantum mechanics a
bound of $2\sqrt{2}$ (the Tsirelson
bound~\cite{cirelson:80}), and the no-signaling principle an algebraic
limit of~4.
The three models are (see Fig.~\ref{fig:correlations}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Classical (linear \blue{/ ``triangle'' model}):}
  \[
    E_c(\theta) = -1 + \frac{2\theta}{\pi}\qquad (0\le \theta \le \pi),
  \]
  \blue{with the extension to other angles understood by the usual symmetries.
  This is a standard \emph{rotationally covariant} local-hidden-variable
  (LHV) correlation satisfying the singlet boundary condition $E(0)=-1$
  (perfect anti-correlation at zero misalignment) and unbiased marginals.}
  For the \blue{conventional CHSH choice of settings used in this work}, it
  attains the local bound $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{CHSH}}^{(c)}=2$.\blue{\footnote{%
  The robustness hierarchy established here is intended to compare
  correlations within the class that is natural for singlet-type Bell
  experiments, namely rotationally covariant correlations with unbiased
  marginals and the singlet boundary condition $E(0)=-1$.
  If one relaxes these constraints, there exist classical shared-randomness
  strategies (e.g., with $E(\theta)=\mathrm{const}$) that can yield a flat
  work profile; such strategies lie outside our comparison class.}}
    \item \textbf{Quantum (cosine):}
      $E_q(\theta) = -\cos\theta$.
      This describes measurements on a two-qubit singlet state and
      reaches the Tsirelson bound,
      $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{CHSH}}^{(q)} = 2\sqrt{2}$.
    \item \textbf{Stronger-than-quantum (step):}
      $E_s(\theta) = \operatorname{sgn}(2\theta/\pi - 1)$.
      This hypothetical ``box'' correlation saturates the algebraic
      limit,
      $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{CHSH}}^{(s)} = 4$~\cite{pop-rohr,%
      svozil-krenn}.
      \blue{We note that the standard Popescu--Rohrlich box is
      defined for finitely many settings; the extension to a
      continuous function $E(\theta)$ is an additional idealization
      consistent with no-signaling but not uniquely fixed by the
      PR-box definition.}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Peak work and mutual information}

The thermodynamic value of these correlations is governed by the mutual
information $I_\theta(A{:}E)$ derived in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Itheta}.
As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:mutual_information}, perfect correlation or
anti-correlation ($|E(\theta)|=1$) yields the maximal mutual
information $I=\ln 2$.
Consequently, according to Eq.~\eqref{eq:work_from_I}, the maximum
work extractable per bipartite system \blue{in this binary-outcome,
uniform-marginal setting} is bounded by
$k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2$.
This peak value is achievable in all three models, for instance, by
aligning the measurement bases ($\theta=0$).

\subsection{Robustness analysis}
\label{sec:robustness}

This thermodynamic equivalence in peak yield belies a critical
operational difference: robustness.
The practical advantage of non-classical correlations becomes apparent
when considering misalignment from the optimal setting ($\theta=0$).

\paragraph{Classical (linear) robustness.}
For the classical model,
$E_c(\delta\theta) = -1 + 2\delta\theta / \pi$ near $\theta = 0$.
\blue{Setting $p = (1 + E_c)/2 = \delta\theta / \pi$, the binary entropy
for small $p$ behaves as
$h_2(p) \approx -p \ln p + p + \mathcal{O}(p^2)$.
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
I_c(\delta\theta)
  = \ln 2 - h_2\!\left(\frac{\delta\theta}{\pi}\right)
  \approx \ln 2
    - \frac{\delta\theta}{\pi}
      \ln\!\frac{\pi}{\delta\theta}
    - \frac{\delta\theta}{\pi}
    + \cdots
\label{eq:Ic_expansion}
\end{equation}
The leading correction is
$\Theta(\delta\theta\ln(1/\delta\theta))$---worse than linear}:
the mutual information degrades
sharply for any nonzero misalignment.
This makes the classical resource \emph{brittle} with respect to
experimental noise.

\paragraph{Quantum (cosine) robustness.}
For the quantum model,
\blue{$E_q(\delta\theta) = -\cos\delta\theta
  \approx -1 + \delta\theta^2/2$.
Setting
$p = (1 + E_q)/2 \approx \delta\theta^2/4$, we have
$h_2(p) \approx -p\ln p + p
       \approx \frac{\delta\theta^2}{4}
              \ln\!\frac{4}{\delta\theta^2}
             + \frac{\delta\theta^2}{4}$
for small $\delta\theta$.
More precisely, expanding Eq.~\eqref{eq:Itheta} with
$E_q = -\cos\theta$ we can write
\begin{equation}
I_q(\delta\theta)
  = \ln 2 - h_2\!\left(\sin^2\!\frac{\delta\theta}{2}\right)
  \approx \ln 2
    - \Theta(\delta\theta^2 \ln(1/\delta\theta)).
\label{eq:Iq_expansion}
\end{equation}
The leading correction is \emph{quadratic} (up to logarithmic
factors).}
The quantum correlation $E_q(\theta) = -\cos\theta$ has a
\blue{stationary point} at $\theta = 0$
\blue{(i.e., $E_q'(0) = \sin 0 = 0$)},
which is the fundamental reason for this
improved robustness.
The quantum resource is significantly more \emph{resilient} against
small imperfections in establishing a shared reference frame.

\paragraph{Stronger-than-quantum (step) robustness.}
The stronger-than-quantum model provides the ultimate in robustness:
for any $\delta\theta \neq \pi/2$, we have
$|E_s(\delta\theta)| = 1$ and therefore
$I_s(\delta\theta) = \ln 2$.
Coordination of measurement settings is almost entirely unnecessary;
the resource is maximal for all but a single critical angle.

\paragraph{General criterion.}
The hierarchy above is not an accident of the three specific models:
it reflects a general principle.
For \emph{any} correlation function $E(\theta)$ satisfying
$E(0) = -1$, the robustness of the mutual information $I_\theta$ near
$\theta = 0$ is determined by the \blue{order of the leading nonzero
term in the expansion of $1 + E(\theta)$ around the extremum}:
\blue{\begin{enumerate}
\item If $E(\theta) = -1 + c\,\theta + o(\theta)$ with $c > 0$
  (nonzero first-order coefficient, as for the linear model),
  $I(\delta\theta)$ drops away from
  $\ln 2$ at a rate $\Theta(\delta\theta\ln(1/\delta\theta))$,
  worse than linear.
\item If $E(\theta) = -1 + c\,\theta^2 + o(\theta^2)$ with
  $c > 0$ (vanishing first-order coefficient, i.e., a stationary point
  as for the cosine correlation), the mutual information
  decays only quadratically (up to logarithmic factors):
  $\Theta(\delta\theta^2\ln(1/\delta\theta))$.
  This applies to any quantum state whose correlation function has a
  smooth extremum.
\item If $|E(\theta)| = 1$ is maintained over a finite neighborhood
  of $\theta = 0$, the resource is locally flat and perfectly robust.
\end{enumerate}
More generally, if
$E(\theta) = -1 + c\,|\theta|^n + o(|\theta|^n)$ with $c > 0$ and
$n > 0$, the degradation scales as
$\Theta(|\theta|^n \ln(1/|\theta|))$.}
Thus, the advantage of quantum correlations in this thermodynamic
setting is not tied to the singlet state alone but is generic for any
quantum correlation function \blue{whose first-order coefficient
vanishes at its
extremum}---a direct consequence of the smoothness guaranteed by the
Born rule and unitary evolution.

\blue{\section{Multi-Setting Thermodynamic Robustness}
\label{sec:CHSH_work}}
% =====================================================

The operational hierarchy established above can be quantified by
\blue{an aggregate robustness parameter that evaluates the total
thermodynamic value across multiple measurement settings, as would
arise in a protocol where the measurement angle varies across runs.}

\blue{We adopt the standard CHSH measurement configuration---Alice's
settings $\alpha_1 = 0$, $\alpha_2 = \pi/2$ and Bob's settings
$\beta_1 = \pi/4$, $\beta_2 = -\pi/4$---which produces four
setting pairs with relative angles
$\theta_{11} = \pi/4$, $\theta_{12} = \pi/4$,
$\theta_{21} = \pi/4$, $\theta_{22} = 3\pi/4$.
For isotropic correlations satisfying $|E(\theta)| = |E(\pi-\theta)|$,
one has $I(\pi/4) = I(3\pi/4)$, so the four terms all take the same
value.}

\blue{We define the aggregate thermodynamic robustness parameter}
\begin{equation}
\blue{\mathcal{S}_W
  = k_{\mathrm{B}}T\sum_{k=1}^{4}
    I(\theta_k)
  = k_{\mathrm{B}}T\bigl[3\,I(\pi/4) + I(3\pi/4)\bigr].}
\label{eq:SW_def}
\end{equation}

\blue{%
\emph{Important remark.}
Although $\mathcal{S}_W$ is evaluated at the CHSH measurement angles,
it is \emph{not} a Bell inequality: no local bound is derived or
claimed.
The standard CHSH functional depends on the \emph{signs} of the
correlators through the combination
$E + E + E - E$, which is what enables the separation between local
and nonlocal theories.
The mutual information $I(\theta)$ depends only on $|E(\theta)|$
and is therefore insensitive to the sign structure.
We use $\mathcal{S}_W$ purely as an operational figure of merit
quantifying how much total work can be extracted when measurement
settings span the CHSH configuration.
The hierarchy
$\mathcal{S}_W^{(c)} < \mathcal{S}_W^{(q)} <
\mathcal{S}_W^{(s)}$
reflects the robustness advantage of non-classical correlations:
stronger correlations maintain a higher $|E|$ (and hence higher $I$)
across diverse measurement angles.}

We now evaluate $\mathcal{S}_W$ explicitly for each model.

\paragraph{Classical:}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:Itheta} with $E_c(\pi/4) = -1/2$ and
$E_c(3\pi/4) = 1/2$, so that $|E| = 1/2$ in both cases,
we obtain $I(\pi/4) = I(3\pi/4) = \ln 2 - h_2(1/4)$.
\blue{Computing $h_2(1/4)$ explicitly:
\begin{align}
h_2\left(\tfrac{1}{4}\right) &= -\tfrac{1}{4}\ln\tfrac{1}{4}
             -\tfrac{3}{4}\ln\tfrac{3}{4} \notag\\
          &= 2\ln 2 - \tfrac{3}{4}\ln 3
           \approx 0.5623 \;\text{nats}.
\label{eq:h2_quarter}
\end{align}
Therefore
$I(\pi/4) = \ln 2 - (2\ln 2 - \tfrac{3}{4}\ln 3)
           = \tfrac{3}{4}\ln 3 - \ln 2
           \approx 0.1308$~nats, and}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_W^{(c)}
  = \blue{4 \times 0.1308\, k_{\mathrm{B}}T
  \approx 0.523\, k_{\mathrm{B}}T.}
\label{eq:SW_classical}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Quantum:}
With $E_q(\pi/4) = -\cos(\pi/4) = -1/\sqrt{2}$ and
$E_q(3\pi/4) = -\cos(3\pi/4) = 1/\sqrt{2}$, we similarly have
$|E| = 1/\sqrt{2}$ and
$I(\pi/4) = I(3\pi/4)
  = \ln 2 - h_2\!\bigl(\tfrac{1+1/\sqrt{2}}{2}\bigr)$,
\blue{where $p = (1-1/\sqrt{2})/2 = \sin^2(\pi/8) \approx 0.1464$.
Computing $h_2(0.1464)$ directly:
\begin{align}
h_2(0.1464) &= -0.1464\ln(0.1464) - 0.8536\ln(0.8536)
\notag\\
  &\approx 0.2813 + 0.1352 \notag\\
  &\approx 0.4165~\text{nats}.
\label{eq:h2_quantum}
\end{align}
Therefore $I(\pi/4) \approx 0.6931 - 0.4165
  = 0.2766$~nats, and}
\begin{equation}
\blue{\mathcal{S}_W^{(q)}
  = 4 \times 0.2766\, k_{\mathrm{B}}T
  \approx 1.107\, k_{\mathrm{B}}T.}
\label{eq:SW_quantum}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Stronger-than-quantum:}
With $|E_s(\pi/4)| = |E_s(3\pi/4)| = 1$, we have
$I(\pi/4) = I(3\pi/4) = \ln 2 \approx 0.6931$~nats.
Therefore,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_W^{(s)}
  = \blue{4\,k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2
  \approx 2.773\, k_{\mathrm{B}}T.}
\label{eq:SW_superquantum}
\end{equation}

These results confirm the strict hierarchy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}_W^{(c)} < \mathcal{S}_W^{(q)}
  < \mathcal{S}_W^{(s)}.
\end{equation}
\blue{This hierarchy is a direct consequence of the robustness
analysis of Sec.~\ref{sec:robustness}: stronger non-locality produces
correlations whose magnitude $|E(\theta)|$ is maintained at larger
misalignment angles, resulting in greater aggregate thermodynamic value
across diverse measurement settings.}

\blue{\section{Extensions and Limitations}
\label{sec:extensions}}
% =====================================================

\blue{\subsection{Relaxing the uniform-marginal assumption}
\label{sec:non_uniform}}

\blue{The derivation of the mutual information in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Itheta}
relied on two structural assumptions: (i)~binary outcomes and
(ii)~uniform marginals ($P(a) = P(e) = 1/2$).
Assumption~(ii) can be relaxed straightforwardly.
For a general binary no-signaling distribution with marginals
$P(a{=}+1) = q_A$ and $P(e{=}+1) = q_E$, the mutual information
becomes
\begin{equation}
I(A{:}E) = H(A) + H(E) - H(A,E),
\label{eq:I_general_binary}
\end{equation}
where all entropies are now Shannon entropies of the respective
distributions.
With non-uniform marginals, the joint distribution is no longer
determined by $E(\theta)$ alone: one additionally needs the marginals.
The work bound~\eqref{eq:work_from_I} remains valid, but the simple
formula~\eqref{eq:Itheta} is replaced by a more complex expression
involving two parameters.
For binary outcomes, the mutual information is bounded by
$I(A{:}E) \le \min\{H(A), H(E)\} \le \ln 2$, so the peak
extractable work remains at most $k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2$ regardless
of the marginals.
With non-uniform marginals, $\min\{H(A), H(E)\} < \ln 2$, so the
achievable peak may be \emph{lower} than in the uniform case.

Importantly, the \emph{robustness hierarchy} is unaffected by this
generalization.
The key argument (Sec.~\ref{sec:robustness}, ``General criterion'')
depends only on the leading-order scaling of $1 + E(\theta)$
near its extremum, which
is a property of the correlation law, not of the marginals.
A smooth $E(\theta)$ produces a smooth $I(\theta)$, regardless of the
marginal distribution, and the Taylor expansion around the peak retains
the same leading order.}

\blue{\subsection{Beyond binary outcomes}
\label{sec:beyond_binary}}

\blue{For $d$-outcome measurements, the joint distribution is no longer
captured by a single correlation function, and the conditional entropy
involves the full $d \times d$ joint probability table.
The extractable work is still bounded by
$k_{\mathrm{B}}T\, I(A{:}E)$, but the connection to a single
``angle'' becomes less direct.
A natural generalization replaces $E(\theta)$ by a family of
correlation witnesses (such as the
Collins--Gisin--Linden--Massar--Popescu inequality for
$d>2$~\cite{CGLMP2002}), and the smoothness-based robustness argument
extends to each such witness.
A detailed treatment of the $d>2$ case is beyond the scope of this
work but constitutes a natural direction for future research.}

\blue{\subsection{Single-shot corrections}
\label{sec:single_shot}}

\blue{The work bound~\eqref{eq:work_from_I} is an asymptotic (many-copy, or
thermodynamic-limit) result.
In the single-shot regime, the relevant quantities are the min- and
max-entropies, and the corresponding free
energies~\cite{Brandao2015b,Horodecki2013}.
The single-shot extractable work is
\begin{equation}
W_{\mathrm{ext}}^{(1)}
  \le k_{\mathrm{B}}T\bigl[
    \ln 2 - H_{\min}(A|E)_\rho\bigr],
\end{equation}
where $H_{\min}(A|E)_\rho$ is the conditional min-entropy
(in nats, consistent with the natural-logarithm convention used
throughout this work).
The smoothness argument for the robustness hierarchy remains
applicable, since $H_{\min}$ is a continuous function of the state,
and its dependence on $\theta$ is inherited from the smoothness of
$E(\theta)$.
However, the quantitative values of the aggregate robustness
parameter
$\mathcal{S}_W$ would be modified, and the three-model hierarchy,
while expected to persist qualitatively, deserves independent
verification in the single-shot setting.}

\blue{\subsection{Connection to device-independent protocols}
\label{sec:DI}}

\blue{An analogous robustness advantage of quantum (and hypothetical
beyond-quantum) correlations appears in device-independent quantum key
distribution~\cite{acin2007} and certified randomness
expansion~\cite{acin2012}, where violation of a Bell inequality
certifies the security of a protocol even when the measurement devices
are imperfect.
The thermodynamic robustness identified here---quantum correlations
degrade more gracefully under misalignment---is a manifestation of the
same structural feature: the smoothness of quantum correlations at
their extrema provides intrinsic noise tolerance that is absent in
classical theories and maximally present in no-signaling theories.}

% =====================================================
\blue{\section{Discussion and Relation to Prior Work}
\label{sec:discussion}}
% =====================================================

\blue{Our results complement and extend several strands of the literature on
correlations as thermodynamic resources.

The foundational connection between correlations and work was
established by Oppenheim~\emph{et al.}~\cite{Oppenheim2002}, who
introduced the ``work deficit''---the gap between globally and locally
extractable work---as a measure of quantum correlations.
While the work deficit quantifies how much correlation \emph{is}
present, our analysis addresses a different question: how
\emph{robust} a given correlation is as a fuel.
The two perspectives are complementary.

Zurek~\cite{Zurek2003} connected quantum discord to the work
extractable by Maxwell's demon, showing that the ``quantumness'' of
correlations has thermodynamic consequences.
Our work extends this perspective beyond quantum mechanics to
include hypothetical no-signaling correlations, and it identifies
robustness---rather than the absolute amount of extractable work---as
the key distinguishing feature.

Perarnau-Llobet~\emph{et al.}~\cite{PerarnauLlobet2015} provided a
comprehensive framework for extractable work from general correlated
states, proving tight bounds for local and global extraction protocols.
Our work specializes to the Bell-experiment scenario and reveals a
hierarchy that is not visible in bounds based solely on the amount of
correlation.

Funo \emph{et al.}~\cite{funo2013} showed that entanglement can
provide a thermodynamic advantage in work extraction under global
operations, while Hovhannisyan \emph{et al.}~\cite{hovhannisyan2013}
demonstrated that entanglement generation is not \emph{necessary} for
optimal work extraction by local operations.
Our finding that $W_{\max} = k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2$ for all three
correlation classes is consistent with the latter result:
the peak work is limited by the communicated information, not the
nonlocality of the correlations.
The advantage of nonlocality lies instead in the robustness with which
this peak can be approached.

Gallego \emph{et al.}~\cite{gallego2010} studied operational
advantages of nonlocal correlations in information-theoretic tasks.
Our work provides a thermodynamic analog of such advantages: the
operational advantage is not a higher yield but a greater tolerance to
imprecision.

The resource-theoretic approach to
thermodynamics~\cite{Brandao2015,Brandao2015b,Horodecki2013}
introduces families of generalized free energies and establishes
``second laws'' beyond the standard one.
Our bound in Eq.~\eqref{eq:work_from_I} is the asymptotic
(many-copy) version of these constraints; the single-shot corrections
(see Sec.~\ref{sec:single_shot}) would modify the quantitative values
of $\mathcal{S}_W$ but not the qualitative robustness hierarchy, which
is determined by the smoothness of~$E(\theta)$.

It is important to clarify the scope of our results. The bound in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:work_from_I} is standard within information thermodynamics:
for systems coupled to a heat bath at temperature $T$, under the usual
assumptions of the generalized second law, the maximum average work
extractable in a cyclic protocol is bounded by $k_{\mathrm{B}}T$ times the
(appropriate) mutual information. In our operational setting we apply this
to the classical outcome registers generated by the parties' measurements.
The expression for $I_\theta$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:Itheta} is similarly
model-independent: it follows solely from assuming binary outcomes and
uniform marginals, together with the correlator $E(\theta)=\langle
AE\rangle_\theta$.
What is model-dependent is the specific functional form of $E(\theta)$:
the linear function is a standard (and, within a natural isotropic LHV
comparison class, extremal) rotationally covariant local model; the cosine
arises from the singlet state; and the step function is an idealized
no-signaling form reaching $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{CHSH}}=4$~\cite{svozil-krenn}.
However, as argued in Sec.~\ref{sec:robustness}, the robustness
hierarchy depends only on the leading-order scaling of
$E(\theta)$ at its extrema and is therefore generic within each theory.
We emphasize that the robustness comparison is made within the
class of rotationally covariant correlations satisfying $E(0) = -1$;
outside this class (e.g., constant classical correlations that ignore
the angle) the hierarchy does not apply.}

% =====================================================
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================

Correlations between a system and its environment are a thermodynamic
fuel, \blue{whose value in our binary-outcome, uniform-marginal
setting is} limited by their mutual information.
A single perfectly correlated bit can yield at most
$k_{\mathrm{B}}T\ln 2$ of work, as exemplified by a Szilard engine
powered by shared information~\cite{Szilard-1929}.
The hierarchy among classical, quantum, and stronger-than-quantum
correlations lies not in this peak value but in the robustness with
which it can be accessed.
Quantum correlations provide a distinct operational advantage over
classical ones through their resilience to misalignment, a feature
directly linked to \blue{the smoothness of their correlation function
at its extremum}.
Engines consuming correlations do not violate the second law; they
demonstrate that information must be treated as a physical
resource~\cite{landauer:61,bennett-82}.
\blue{The robustness hierarchy---classical $<$ quantum $<$
no-signaling---provides a new thermodynamic signature that parallels
the CHSH correlation hierarchy, and suggests that the ``cost'' of
stronger correlations is not energetic but metrological: classical
strategies demand precise alignment, quantum strategies are more
forgiving, and no-signaling strategies are maximally tolerant.
This hierarchy is established within the natural comparison class of
rotationally covariant correlations satisfying the singlet boundary
condition.}
