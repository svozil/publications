\title{Chromatic Completeness and the Independence of Geometric Obstruction}

\author{Author Name}
\affiliation{Affiliation, Address}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
We establish a strict logical separation between two distinct phenomena in
the theory of orthogonality hypergraphs:
\emph{chromatic completeness}---the ability to assign a globally consistent
spectrum to maximal observables across all contexts---and
\emph{geometric coordinatizability}---the existence of a faithful orthogonal
representation (FOR).
While Shekarriz and Svozil observed that a chromatic number $\chi > n$
obstructs chromatic completeness in dimension~$n$, this condition does not
constrain the existence of an FOR.
We show that spectral assignments constitute an additional layer of
structure on top of geometric realizations; consequently, chromatic
obstructions constrain the spectral labeling, not the underlying geometry.
The Yu--Oh configuration ($\chi=4$) provides an explicit counterexample to
any implication from chromatic number to geometric
non-coordinatizability.
Conversely, to demonstrate that geometric obstruction is an independent
phenomenon, we provide an elementary algebraic proof that Greechie's
$G_{32}$ hypergraph admits no faithful orthogonal representation in
$\mathbb{C}^3$, a result derived solely from the incidence geometry of its
rays without reference to its chromatic properties.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An orthogonality hypergraph $H=(V,E)$ consists of a vertex set~$V$ and a
collection~$E$ of hyperedges (contexts), each comprising $n$ mutually
orthogonal rank-one projectors in a prospective Hilbert space
$\mathcal{H}^n$.
A \emph{faithful orthogonal representation} (FOR) is a geometric
realization: an assignment of distinct rays
$[v_i] \in \mathbb{P}(\mathcal{H}^n)$ to vertices such that orthogonality
within every hyperedge is preserved.
The existence of such representations is the central question in the
quantum logic of the Kochen--Specker theorem~\cite{Kochen1967}.

Recent work by Shekarriz and Svozil~\cite{Shekarriz2022, Svozil2025}
introduced \emph{chromatic contextuality}, observing that hypergraphs with
chromatic number $\chi(H) > n$ cannot support representations where every
context carries the same fixed spectrum.
This has led to a subtle conflation in the literature: the impression that
$\chi > n$ obstructs the existence of an FOR \emph{tout court}.

The primary result of this paper is that such a conflation mistakes a
property of the \emph{spectral labeling} for a property of the
\emph{underlying geometry}.
We formalize the notion of \emph{chromatic completeness}
(Definition~\ref{def:chromatic-completeness}) as the requirement that an FOR
admit a global labeling of vertices by eigenvalues such that each context
realizes the same spectrum.
This is a constraint on the \emph{spectral assignment}, not on the
\emph{geometry}.
We prove:

\begin{proposition}[Independence]\label{prop:independence}
The existence of an FOR in $\mathcal{H}^n$ is independent of the chromatic
number $\chi(H)$. Specifically:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] If $\chi(H) \leq n$, an FOR may or may not exist;
    \item[(ii)] If $\chi(H) > n$, an FOR may or may not exist.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

The first clause is trivial; the second is established by the Yu--Oh configuration~\cite{Yu2012}, which has $\chi=4$ yet possesses a well-known FOR in $\mathbb{R}^3$. Thus chromatic bounds govern only the existence of \emph{chromatically complete} representations. The proof that $G_{32}$ is nevertheless non-coordinatizable despite having the same chromatic number shows that geometric obstruction is a separate phenomenon requiring direct geometric analysis.


To close the logical loop, we address the converse question: if chromatic
properties do not forbid geometric realizability, what does?
We treat Greechie's $G_{32}$ hypergraph~\cite{Greechie1971}---which has
$\chi=4$---as a case study in pure geometric obstruction.
By an explicit algebraic argument, we demonstrate that $G_{32}$ admits no
faithful orthogonal representation in $\mathbb{C}^3$.
The obstruction arises from the rigid incidence geometry of the
configuration, manifesting as an algebraic identity that forces distinct
vertices to occupy identical rays.
This establishes that $G_{32}$ is non-coordinatizable for reasons entirely
distinct from its chromatic number.

\section{Spectral Assignments and Geometric Realizability}

To clarify the relationship between geometry and colorings, we introduce the
following structure.
Let $\mathcal{R}(H,\mathcal{H}^n)$ denote the set of faithful orthogonal
representations of $H$ in $\mathcal{H}^n$ (the \emph{realization space}).
For a fixed representation $\rho \in \mathcal{R}$, each hyperedge $e\in E$
corresponds to an orthonormal basis $\{[v_i]\}_{i\in e}$.
A maximal observable for this context is any operator
$A_e = \sum_{i\in e}\lambda_i P_i$ with distinct eigenvalues.

\begin{definition}[Chromatic Completeness]\label{def:chromatic-completeness}
A representation $\rho$ is \emph{chromatically complete} if there exists a
single set $\Sigma = \{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n\}$ of distinct scalars and
a function $c\colon V\to\Sigma$ such that for every context
$e = \{v_{i_1},\dots,v_{i_n}\}$, the restriction $c|_e$ is a bijection
onto~$\Sigma$.
\end{definition}

In other words, chromatic completeness requires the eigenvalue assignment to
be a proper $n$-coloring of the hypergraph.
The observation of Shekarriz and Svozil is that $\chi(H)>n$ implies no
chromatically complete representation exists.
This is correct, but it constrains only the \emph{spectral labeling}
associated with~$\rho$, not the geometry.

Indeed, fix a geometric realization~$\rho$.
The set of possible spectral assignments for a single context~$e$ is the
symmetric group~$S_n$ (permuting the $n$ eigenvalues onto the $n$ rays).
A chromatically complete representation requires a globally consistent
choice of eigenvalue permutations across all contexts---equivalently, a
proper $n$-coloring.
The Yu--Oh configuration demonstrates that such a globally consistent
assignment may not exist (no 3-coloring exists) while the geometric
realization space $\mathcal{R}$ is nonetheless nonempty.

The physical irrelevance of chromatic completeness follows from the
functional calculus.
Two maximal operators sharing the same spectral projections differ only by a relabeling of their outcomes.

\begin{lemma}[Spectral Equivalence]\label{lem:spectral}
Let
\[
A = \sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i P_i
\]
be a maximal observable on $\mathcal{H}^n$, where the spectral projections
$\{P_i\}$ are one-dimensional and the eigenvalues $a_i$ are distinct.
Then every maximal observable sharing the same spectral projections is of
the form
\[
B = f(A),
\]
where $f$ is injective on the spectrum of~$A$.
Conversely, every injective function on the spectrum of~$A$ produces a
maximal observable with the same eigenspaces.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose
\[
B = \sum_{i=1}^{n} b_i P_i
\]
shares the same spectral projections as~$A$.
Since $A$ and $B$ possess the same eigenspaces, they commute and are
simultaneously diagonalizable.
The spectral theorem therefore implies that $B$ is obtained by applying a
function to the eigenvalues of~$A$.

Define
\[
f(a_i) = b_i,\qquad i=1,\ldots,n.
\]
Because both observables are maximal (non-degenerate), the numbers $b_i$
are distinct, so $f$ is injective on the finite spectrum of~$A$.

An explicit realization of $f$ is given by the Lagrange interpolation
polynomial
\[
f(x)
=
\sum_{i=1}^{n}
b_i
\prod_{\substack{j=1\\j\neq i}}^{n}
\frac{x - a_j}{a_i - a_j},
\]
which satisfies $f(a_i)=b_i$ for every eigenvalue.
Consequently, $B = f(A)$.

Conversely, any injective map on the spectrum preserves the distinctness
of the eigenvalues while leaving the spectral projections unchanged,
producing another maximal observable with the same eigenspaces.
\end{proof}

\section{The Yu--Oh Configuration: Geometric Realizability Without
Chromatic Completeness}

The separation asserted in
Proposition~\ref{prop:independence}(ii) is instantiated by the Yu--Oh
hypergraph $H_{\text{YO}}$ which
has has a vertex set $\{1,\dots,25\}$ and 16 blocks~\cite{Yu2012}:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\{   1,  2,  3 \}, \,
 \{   4,  5,  6 \}, \,
 \{   7,  8,  9 \}, \,
 \{   2,  5,  8 \}, \,
\\
&
 \{   9, 10, 23 \}, \,
 \{   4, 11, 24 \}, \,
 \{   3, 12, 25 \}, \,
 \{   1, 13, 20 \}, \,
\\
&
 \{   6, 13, 22 \}, \,
 \{   7, 13, 21 \}, \,
 \{   3, 11, 14 \}, \,
 \{   7, 11, 15 \}, \,
\\
&
 \{   9, 12, 16 \}, \,
 \{   6, 12, 17 \}, \,
 \{   4, 10, 18 \}, \,
 \{   1, 10, 19 \}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
It is 3-uniform (all contexts contain 3 vertices) and has chromatic number
$\chi(H_{\text{YO}})=4$.

If chromatic number obstructed geometric realizability, $H_{\text{YO}}$
would admit no FOR in $\mathbb{R}^3$.
However, an explicit coordinatization exists~\cite{Yu2012}.
These 13 distinct rays realize all 9 contexts of $H_{\text{YO}}$ with
mutual orthogonality preserved.

The non-existence of a 3-coloring implies that no choice of maximal
operators on this geometry can share a common spectrum.
But by Lemma~\ref{lem:spectral}, we may simply assign independent spectra
to distinct contexts.
For instance, context $C_1$ may carry eigenvalues $\{0,1,2\}$ while an
overlapping context $C_2$ carries $\{0,\pi,e\}$; the shared projector
receives different numerical labels in each context, related by an
injective remapping.
The Yu--Oh configuration thus demonstrates that the chromatic obstruction
is confined to the spectral labeling and does not affect the existence of
the geometric realization.

\section{Geometric Obstruction in $G_{32}$}

Having established that chromatic contextuality does not preclude geometric
realizability, we now demonstrate that geometric obstruction is
nevertheless a genuine and distinct phenomenon.
We prove by elementary algebraic methods that Greechie's diagram $G_{32}$
admits no faithful orthogonal representation in $\mathbb{C}^3$.

\subsection{The hypergraph}
The Greechie diagram or hypergraph $G_{32}$ has vertex set $\{1,\dots,15\}$ and 10 blocks
(triples of mutually orthogonal rays):
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
&\{1,2,3\},\, \{3,4,5\},\, \{5,6,7\},\, \{7,8,9\},\,
\{9,10,11\},\\
&\{11,12,1\},\, \{4,10,13\},\, \{6,12,14\},\, \{8,2,15\},\,
\{13,14,15\}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Main theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:g32}
The hypergraph $G_{32}$ admits no faithful orthogonal representation in
$\mathbb{C}^3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Assume an FOR exists.
We derive two independent algebraic constraints on the transition
amplitudes between certain vertices, which together force a contradiction
with faithfulness.

\paragraph{Step 1: Gauge fixing.}
Since $\{13,14,15\}$ is a block, its vertices form an orthonormal basis.
By a global unitary transformation, we fix:
\[
v_{13}=e_1=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\\0\end{pmatrix},\;
v_{14}=e_2=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\\0\end{pmatrix},\;
v_{15}=e_3=\begin{pmatrix}0\\0\\1\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\paragraph{Step 2: Spoke parametrization.}
The three spoke contexts $\{4,10,13\}$, $\{6,12,14\}$, and $\{8,2,15\}$
connect the central triangle to the outer cycle.
Each spoke forces two vectors to lie in a coordinate two-plane, reducing
the problem to three pairs of complex parameters.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Block $\{4,10,13\}$:} $v_4,v_{10}\perp e_1$, so they lie in
$\spanop(e_2,e_3)$.
Write
\[
v_4 = \begin{pmatrix}0\\ a\\ b\end{pmatrix},\quad |a|^2+|b|^2=1,
\]
and take the unique orthogonal ray in this plane as
\[
v_{10} = \begin{pmatrix}0\\ -\overline{b}\\ \overline{a}\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\item \emph{Block $\{6,12,14\}$:} $v_6,v_{12}\perp e_2$, lying in
$\spanop(e_1,e_3)$:
\[
v_6 = \begin{pmatrix}c\\ 0\\ d\end{pmatrix},\quad
v_{12} = \begin{pmatrix}-\overline{d}\\ 0\\ \overline{c}\end{pmatrix},
\quad |c|^2+|d|^2=1.
\]

\item \emph{Block $\{8,2,15\}$:} $v_8,v_2\perp e_3$, lying in
$\spanop(e_1,e_2)$:
\[
v_2 = \begin{pmatrix}p\\ q\\ 0\end{pmatrix},\quad
v_8 = \begin{pmatrix}-\overline{q}\\ \overline{p}\\ 0\end{pmatrix},
\quad |p|^2+|q|^2=1.
\]
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Step 3: A projection identity.}
The key technical ingredient is a simple consequence of the resolution of
the identity.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:projection}
If $\{x,y,z\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathbb{C}^3$, and $u\perp x$,
$w\perp z$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projection}
\langle u,w\rangle = \langle u,y\rangle\,\langle y,w\rangle.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Expanding
\[
w = \langle x,w\rangle\, x + \langle y,w\rangle\, y + \langle z,w\rangle\, z,
\]
the last coefficient vanishes because $w\perp z$, while taking the inner
product with $u$ eliminates the first because $u\perp x$.
Only the middle term survives.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Step 4: Two forced transition-amplitude identities.}
The outer twelve-cycle provides two independent propagation paths.
Applying the projection identity~\eqref{eq:projection} along opposite
halves of the cycle yields two factorizations of transition amplitudes
between the spoke vectors.

\emph{Identity~A:} Using block $\{1,2,3\}$ as an orthonormal basis
$\{v_3,v_2,v_1\}$ (with $v_3\perp v_4$ from block $\{3,4,5\}$ and
$v_1\perp v_{12}$ from block $\{11,12,1\}$),
Eq.~\eqref{eq:projection} gives:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identityA}
\langle v_4,v_{12}\rangle = \langle v_4,v_2\rangle\,\langle v_2,v_{12}\rangle.
\end{equation}

\emph{Identity~B:} Using block $\{7,8,9\}$ as an orthonormal basis
$\{v_9,v_8,v_7\}$ (with $v_9\perp v_{10}$ from block $\{9,10,11\}$ and
$v_7\perp v_6$ from block $\{5,6,7\}$):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:identityB}
\langle v_{10},v_6\rangle
= \langle v_{10},v_8\rangle\,\langle v_8,v_6\rangle.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 5: Algebraic evaluation.}
Substituting the explicit parametrizations into the two identities produces
two independent algebraic constraints relating the six free parameters:
\begin{align*}
\langle v_4,v_{12}\rangle &= \overline{b}\,\overline{c}, &
\langle v_4,v_2\rangle &= \overline{a}\,q, &
\langle v_2,v_{12}\rangle &= -\overline{p}\,\overline{d}, \\
\langle v_{10},v_6\rangle &= ad, &
\langle v_{10},v_8\rangle &= -b\,\overline{p}, &
\langle v_8,v_6\rangle &= -qc.
\end{align*}

From \eqref{eq:identityA} we obtain
$\overline{b}\,\overline{c} = -\overline{a}\,\overline{d}\,q\,\overline{p}$,
or upon complex conjugation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constraint1}
bc = -ad\,\overline{q}\,p.
\end{equation}

From \eqref{eq:identityB} we obtain:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:constraint2}
ad = bc\,\overline{p}\,q.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 6: Derivation of the contradiction.}
Substituting~\eqref{eq:constraint1} into~\eqref{eq:constraint2} yields:
\[
ad = (-ad\,\overline{q}\,p)\,\overline{p}\,q
   = -ad\,|p|^2|q|^2.
\]
Since $|p|^2+|q|^2=1$, the factor $1+|p|^2|q|^2$ is strictly positive,
forcing $ad=0$.

If $a=0$, then $|b|=1$ and $v_4=be_3$, implying $[v_4]=[v_{15}]$,
contradicting the distinctness of atoms~4 and~15.

If $d=0$, then $|c|=1$ and $v_6=ce_1$, implying $[v_6]=[v_{13}]$,
contradicting the distinctness of atoms~6 and~13.

Thus no faithful representation exists.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Remark on the nature of the obstruction}
The contradiction arises from the algebraic rigidity of the $G_{32}$
incidence structure, specifically the spoke geometry connecting the outer
twelve-cycle to the central triangle $\{13,14,15\}$.
This is a purely projective-geometric phenomenon: the constraints on inner
products propagate around the cycle to force a ray collision.
It bears no relation to the chromatic number $\chi=4$; indeed, the proof
never invokes coloring arguments.
The obstruction is therefore strictly geometric.

\section{Conclusion}

We have shown that the existence of a faithful orthogonal representation is
logically independent of the chromatic number.
The condition $\chi>n$ obstructs only the \emph{chromatically complete}
representations---those admitting a global spectral labeling---not the
geometric realization itself.
The Yu--Oh configuration provides the definitive counterexample: $\chi=4$
in $\mathbb{R}^3$, yet a faithful representation exists.
Conversely, $G_{32}$ demonstrates that geometric obstruction is a separate,
rigorously demonstrable phenomenon.

In assessing whether a hypergraph admits an FOR, one must therefore look to
the incidence geometry and algebraic dependencies of its rays, not to the
combinatorics of its colorings.
Chromatic contextuality concerns the global consistency of measurement labels, whereas geometric coordinatizability concerns the realizability of measurement directions. These are logically distinct aspects of quantum contextuality and should not be conflated.
